Observability & the Public Goods Game

Does seeing what your peers contributed change how much you contribute?

The rules (same payoff structure as Games and Behavior #79, Week 1):

  • Each round you have 10 tokens. Split them between your Personal Fund and the Group Fund.
  • Every token you keep in your Personal Fund pays you $4.
  • Every token anyone (you or your 4 peers) puts in the Group Fund pays each of the 5 players $2.
  • There are 6 rounds. Rounds 1–3 are Low Observability: you cannot see your peers' individual contributions. Rounds 4–6 are High Observability: before you decide, you will see exactly what each of your 4 peers contributed last round.

Your 4 peers follow a fixed, pre-set schedule (not affected by your choices) so results are reproducible.

Why this matters for SDG 4 (Quality Education):

This game is designed to teach students of economics, computer science, or behavioral science how observability changes cooperation in a public-goods dilemma — a mechanism usually taught only through abstract equations. By playing through Low- and High-Observability rounds yourself, you build direct intuition for whether seeing what your peers contributed changes your own choices, and whether that shift reflects genuine cooperation or just visible compliance. An observable indicator of learning: compare your own contribution in Rounds 1–3 (Low) against Rounds 4–6 (High), and consider why it did or didn't change.
